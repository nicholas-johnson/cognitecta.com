# Linear Horizon Brand Guide

Version 1.0  
Status: Initial digital identity system

---

## 1. Brand idea

**Linear Horizon turns complexity into a clear course of action.**

The identity is built from the simplest mathematical elements: a point, a line, a circle and a boundary. These forms represent the journey from scattered information to understanding, and from understanding to a practical outcome.

The brand should feel:

- Clear, intelligent and assured
- Mathematical without feeling academic
- Corporate without becoming anonymous
- Futuristic without science-fiction styling
- Calm, exact and useful

The brand should never rely on generic AI imagery such as brains, circuit boards, robots, glowing networks, infinity symbols or starbursts.

### Core proposition

> From complexity to outcome.

### Supporting language

- Intelligence, applied.
- Understand. Decide. Act.
- See further. Act sooner.
- Complex problems. Clear outcomes.
- Systems that get work done.

Use **From complexity to outcome.** as the principal external tagline until there is a strong reason to change it.

## 2. The visual grammar

Linear Horizon has four related visual elements. They are not four competing logos; each has a distinct role.

### 2.1 Primary mark: the analytical horizon

The primary logo is a mathematically precise circle bisected by a horizontal line.

- The **circle** represents a system, model, field of knowledge or bounded problem.
- The **horizontal line** represents clarity, direction and the horizon beyond the current system.
- The line extends beyond both sides of the circle, showing that insight crosses boundaries.

This is the default corporate mark for the website header, favicon, social avatar, proposals, presentations and formal material.

#### Construction

Use a 100-unit construction grid:

- Circle diameter: `72 units`
- Circle centre: `(50, 50)`
- Circle stroke: `4 units`
- Horizon line: from `x=4` to `x=96`, at `y=50`
- Horizon stroke: `4 units`
- Stroke caps: square or butt, never rounded
- Circle: graphite
- Horizon: cobalt blue in colour applications
- One-colour version: entirely graphite or white
- No fill inside the circle

The line must pass through the exact optical centre of the circle. Avoid adding a sun, landscape, orbit, arrow or gradient.

#### Clear space and minimum size

- Clear space: at least one quarter of the circle diameter on every side
- Minimum digital size: `20px` high for the symbol alone
- Minimum print size: `8mm` high
- Below `24px`, use the one-colour version and slightly strengthen the strokes if required

### 2.2 Transformation mark: resolution

The secondary mark begins with three separate points and resolves into one continuous horizontal line.

`●  ●  ●  ─────────`

It represents:

- Signals becoming understanding
- Fragmented systems becoming coordinated
- Multiple possibilities becoming a decision
- Analysis becoming action
- Work reaching a completed outcome

Use this mark in hero graphics, loading states, product explanations, section transitions and motion. It should not normally replace the primary circle mark in the site header.

#### Construction

- Three equal circular points
- Point diameter: equal to the line's visual thickness multiplied by `1.5`
- Equal spacing between points
- Gap between the final point and the line: equal to the point spacing
- The points and line share one exact horizontal centre
- In motion, the points may travel, align and merge into the line

### 2.3 Outcome mark: line and square

A horizontal line terminating in a solid square represents delivered work:

`────────────■`

- The line is the process
- The square is the concrete result
- The square must feel like an endpoint, not an arrowhead

Use it for calls to action, progress indicators, dividers, case-study outcomes and the final stage of diagrams.

The square can be cobalt when the line is graphite. Never animate it as a flashing cursor.

### 2.4 Connecting line: the product system

A continuous line moves through the site and connects products, capabilities, evidence and outcomes. It establishes that Linear Horizon is one coherent system rather than a collection of unrelated AI tools.

The line may:

- Enter a section from outside the viewport
- Pass through a product node
- Become a chart axis or diagram edge
- Resolve into the square outcome mark
- Continue subtly from one section to the next

It must remain functional and restrained. It should never become decorative spaghetti or reduce readability.

On mobile, simplify the line to a vertical or short horizontal progression. Do not attempt to preserve a complex desktop path on a small screen.

## 3. Logo lockups

Create and maintain these official variants:

1. **Primary horizontal lockup** — analytical-horizon symbol to the left of the `LINEAR HORIZON` wordmark
2. **Stacked lockup** — symbol above the wordmark for square formats
3. **Symbol only** — circle-and-line mark for favicon, avatar and compact navigation
4. **One-colour dark** — graphite artwork on a light background
5. **One-colour light** — white artwork on a graphite background
6. **Small-size favicon** — optically corrected symbol for 16px and 32px

Do not:

- Rotate or skew the mark
- Put the mark inside another containing shape
- Add shadows, gradients, glow or dimensional effects
- Round the horizon line into a friendly pill
- Stretch the symbol or alter its proportions
- Use the resolution mark as a permanent replacement for the primary logo
- place the blue line on a background with insufficient contrast

## 4. Wordmark and typography

The wordmark is uppercase:

`LINEAR HORIZON`

It should use a neutral grotesk sans serif with generous but controlled tracking. It must feel engineered rather than luxurious or fashionable.

### Recommended digital type system

- Primary family: **Inter**
- Display headings: Inter, weight `500–600`
- Body: Inter, weight `400`
- UI labels: Inter, weight `500`
- Mathematical/data labels: **IBM Plex Mono**, weight `400–500`

If an appropriate licensed grotesk is already used by the company, it may be retained after visual comparison. Do not introduce a typeface merely for novelty.

### Typesetting principles

- Use sentence case for headings and interface copy
- Reserve spaced uppercase for the wordmark and occasional small labels
- Prefer short, declarative headings
- Avoid ultra-light type
- Use tabular numerals for metrics
- Keep line lengths between roughly 55 and 75 characters for long-form copy

## 5. Colour system

### Core palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Graphite 950 | `#15181E` | Primary text, dark surfaces, logo circle |
| Graphite 800 | `#2A2F38` | Secondary dark surface |
| Graphite 600 | `#5D6572` | Secondary text |
| Horizon 500 | `#145BFF` | Horizon line, active states, key data |
| Horizon 600 | `#0047D6` | Accessible blue on light backgrounds |
| Porcelain | `#F7F5EF` | Primary warm background |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and reversed text |
| Rule | `#D9DCE2` | Borders, grids and quiet dividers |

### Usage ratio

- 70% warm white or white
- 20% graphite
- 10% or less cobalt blue

Blue is an analytical signal, not decorative colour. Avoid purple-blue AI gradients and luminous cyan.

### Accessibility

- Normal text must meet WCAG AA contrast of at least `4.5:1`
- Large text and essential graphical elements must meet at least `3:1`
- Never communicate state using blue alone
- Provide visible keyboard focus styles

## 6. Layout and composition

Use a rigorous grid with generous negative space.

- Desktop content grid: 12 columns
- Tablet content grid: 8 columns
- Mobile content grid: 4 columns
- Prefer one strong idea per section
- Align text, diagrams and product nodes to shared axes
- Use rules and lines purposefully
- Avoid excessive cards, floating pills and rounded containers
- Use modest corner radii: typically `0–8px`
- Prefer thin structural borders over shadows

The overall effect should resemble an excellent technical report translated into a modern product website.

---

## 7. Imagery, diagrams and icons

### Preferred imagery

- Real working environments
- Precise system diagrams
- Sparse data visualisations
- Close observational photography
- Evidence of practical outcomes

### Diagram language

Construct diagrams from the same primitives as the identity:

- Points represent signals, actors or inputs
- Circles represent systems or bounded models
- Lines represent relationships or processes
- Squares represent decisions, actions or delivered outcomes

Use cobalt to identify the active path. Supporting possibilities should be graphite, grey or fine dashed lines.

### Iconography

- Geometric outline icons
- Square line caps where possible
- Consistent optical weight
- No filled cartoon icons
- No mixed icon libraries without normalization

## 8. Motion principles

Motion should explain the system.

Good motion:

- Points align and resolve into a line
- Several paths converge into one selected path
- A line travels through product nodes
- The terminal square appears when an outcome is reached
- Charts draw once when they enter the viewport

Avoid:

- Ambient floating particles
- Constant pulsing
- Parallax for its own sake
- Fast or elastic movement
- Glowing trails

Recommended durations:

- UI transitions: `140–220ms`
- Explanatory line motion: `500–900ms`
- Page-level sequence: no more than `1200ms`

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. With reduced motion enabled, display the completed state without travel, drawing or convergence animations.

## 9. Verbal identity

Linear Horizon speaks plainly. It should sound like experienced engineers and advisers who understand the work, not an AI marketing generator.

### Voice

- Direct
- Calm
- Specific
- Evidence-led
- Commercially literate
- Confident without grandiosity

### Prefer

- “We identify where work slows down and build systems that remove the friction.”
- “Understand what is happening. Decide what matters. Automate the next action.”
- “AI systems designed around the way your organisation actually works.”

### Avoid

- “Revolutionary AI-powered solutions”
- “Unlock the limitless power of tomorrow”
- “Cutting-edge digital transformation”
- Unsupported claims about intelligence, autonomy or ROI

### Recommended homepage language

**Eyebrow:** Applied intelligence for real organisations  
**Headline:** From complexity to outcome.  
**Introduction:** Linear Horizon understands how work actually happens, identifies the points of friction, and builds intelligent systems that turn insight into action.  
**Primary CTA:** Explore our systems  
**Secondary CTA:** Talk to us

Products should inherit the authority of the parent brand:

- Linear Horizon Query

When the relationship is already clear, use the short product name in navigation and UI. Use the full endorsed name on product landing pages, proposals and first mentions.

The connecting line can pass through each product node and terminate at a square labelled **Outcome**.

## 12. Brand test

Every new design should pass these questions:

1. Does it make something complex feel clearer?
2. Does every line, point or shape have a function?
3. Could it be understood without a glowing AI cliché?
4. Does it feel credible in front of a bank, government department or global enterprise?
5. Is there a visible relationship between information and outcome?
6. Does it remain legible and useful on mobile and with reduced motion?

If the answer to any of these is no, simplify.

# Appendix A: Cursor Prompt — Rebrand the Existing Website

Copy everything inside the following prompt into Cursor at the root of the existing website repository.

```text
You are updating an existing production website to the new Linear Horizon brand identity.

Your job is to inspect the repository, understand the current framework and design system, produce a short implementation plan, and then implement the rebrand without breaking existing behaviour.

Do not rebuild the site from scratch. Preserve working routes, content structure, forms, analytics, SEO behaviour, accessibility and integrations unless a change is explicitly required below. Reuse good existing components where possible, but remove visual conventions that conflict with the new identity.

BRAND POSITION

Linear Horizon turns organisational complexity into clear, practical outcomes. It understands how work actually happens, identifies friction and builds intelligent systems that transform insight into action.

Principal tagline:
“From complexity to outcome.”

Brand character:
- Clear, intelligent and assured
- Mathematical but not academic
- Corporate but not anonymous
- Future-facing but not science-fiction
- Calm, exact and useful

Avoid generic AI imagery: brains, robots, circuit boards, infinity symbols, glowing neural networks, starbursts, neon gradients and decorative space imagery.

FIRST: AUDIT THE EXISTING SITE

Before editing:
1. Identify the framework, package manager, styling system, component structure and build/test commands.
2. Identify all current brand-name, logo, favicon, colour, font, metadata, structured-data and social-image references.
3. Identify reusable layout and UI components.
4. Identify critical behaviour that must not regress: navigation, responsive layouts, forms, authentication, analytics, consent, tracking, API calls and deployment configuration.
5. Report the files you expect to modify and give a concise implementation plan.
6. Then proceed with the implementation unless the repository contains a genuine ambiguity that could cause destructive changes.

IDENTITY SYSTEM

Use four coordinated mathematical motifs. They have separate roles and must not compete with one another.

1. PRIMARY LOGO — ANALYTICAL HORIZON

Create a clean, accessible, code-native SVG logo component and reusable SVG assets.

The symbol is a precise unfilled circle bisected through its exact centre by a horizontal line extending beyond both sides.

Use a 100-unit viewBox:
- Circle centre: 50,50
- Circle diameter: 72
- Circle radius: 36
- Circle stroke width: 4
- Horizon line: x1=4, y1=50, x2=96, y2=50
- Horizon stroke width: 4
- Use square or butt line caps, not rounded caps
- Circle colour: currentColor or Graphite 950
- Horizon colour: Horizon blue in the primary colour version
- No fills, gradient, glow, shadow or rotation

Create:
- Primary horizontal lockup: mark plus LINEAR HORIZON wordmark
- Stacked lockup
- Symbol-only mark
- One-colour dark version
- One-colour light version
- Optically corrected favicon assets at standard sizes

Use inline SVG components where theming or animation is required. Also export static assets for metadata and external use. SVGs must have appropriate accessible labelling when meaningful and aria-hidden when decorative.

2. SECONDARY MOTIF — RESOLUTION

Create a reusable ResolutionMark component showing three equal points becoming one horizontal line:

●  ●  ●  ─────────

The points and line must share an exact horizontal centre. This represents fragmented signals becoming one clear direction.

Use it in the homepage hero or the first explanatory section. If animated, points should align or converge and resolve into the line once—not pulse continuously.

3. OUTCOME MOTIF — LINE AND SQUARE

Create a reusable OutcomeLine component:

────────────■

The line represents process and the solid square represents a delivered outcome. Use it for calls to action, selected states, case-study outcomes and section endings. The square may be blue while the line is graphite.

Do not make the square look like an arrowhead or animated text cursor.

4. CONNECTING LINE — PRODUCT SYSTEM

Create a restrained responsive ProductLine component or section treatment that visually connects products/capabilities into one system.

The line may:
- Enter from outside a section
- Pass through product nodes
- Become a diagram or chart axis
- Resolve into the square outcome mark

On desktop it may travel horizontally through several aligned product nodes. On mobile, change it to a simple vertical path or short local connectors. Do not force a complex desktop SVG path onto mobile.

If products are already present in the site, preserve their real names and content. If the site needs placeholder architecture, use only these approved names and do not invent product capabilities:
- Query
- Stack
- Signal
- Relay
- Engine

VISUAL DESIGN

Use this palette through semantic design tokens:
- Graphite 950: #15181E
- Graphite 800: #2A2F38
- Graphite 600: #5D6572
- Horizon 500: #145BFF
- Horizon 600: #0047D6
- Porcelain: #F7F5EF
- White: #FFFFFF
- Rule: #D9DCE2

Aim for approximately 70% warm white/white, 20% graphite and no more than 10% cobalt. Blue must behave like a signal, not decoration.

Typography:
- Use Inter for headings, body and UI unless the existing licensed grotesk is demonstrably suitable.
- Use IBM Plex Mono sparingly for mathematical labels, metrics and technical metadata.
- Use medium-weight, sentence-case headings.
- Reserve tracked uppercase for the LINEAR HORIZON wordmark and occasional small labels.
- Avoid ultra-light text.

Layout:
- Use a rigorous responsive grid and generous negative space.
- Prefer one strong idea per section.
- Align content and diagrams to shared axes.
- Prefer fine structural borders over shadows.
- Use modest radii between 0 and 8px.
- Avoid excessive cards, floating pills, glassmorphism and generic SaaS gradients.

HOMEPAGE DIRECTION

Where compatible with the existing content hierarchy, update the homepage opening to:

Eyebrow:
“Applied intelligence for real organisations”

Headline:
“From complexity to outcome.”

Supporting copy:
“Linear Horizon understands how work actually happens, identifies the points of friction, and builds intelligent systems that turn insight into action.”

Primary CTA:
“Explore our systems”

Secondary CTA:
“Talk to us”

Do not overwrite stronger, factually specific existing copy without showing the proposed change. Preserve factual claims and do not invent customers, metrics, capabilities or testimonials.

Use the ResolutionMark as the principal hero graphic. Let its completed line create an alignment or visual handoff into the following section. Use the primary analytical-horizon logo in the navigation.

MOTION

Motion must explain transformation rather than decorate the page.

- UI transitions: 140–220ms
- Explanatory line animation: 500–900ms
- Page-level sequences: maximum 1200ms
- Prefer CSS transforms, opacity and SVG stroke-dash animation
- Avoid layout-triggering animation and unnecessary JavaScript animation dependencies
- Never use continuous ambient particle animation
- Fully support prefers-reduced-motion by showing the completed static state

ACCESSIBILITY AND PERFORMANCE

- Meet WCAG AA colour contrast
- Retain or improve semantic heading order
- Preserve keyboard navigation and strong visible focus states
- Give interactive controls meaningful accessible names
- Decorative SVGs must be aria-hidden
- Meaningful SVGs must have a title or accessible label
- Avoid cumulative layout shift
- Do not add a large animation or component dependency for simple line effects
- Optimize and size all assets appropriately
- Ensure the navigation and primary CTA remain usable without JavaScript where the framework permits

SEO AND BRAND MIGRATION

- Replace user-facing references to the old brand with Linear Horizon where appropriate
- Update document titles, descriptions, Open Graph metadata, social metadata, manifest and structured data
- Update favicon and app-icon references
- Preserve canonical URLs unless a domain migration is explicitly part of this repository
- Do not change legal company names in policies or contracts unless the repository clearly shows that the legal entity has changed
- Do not invent a new domain
- Search again after implementation for accidental old-brand remnants and report intentional legal/history references separately

IMPLEMENTATION QUALITY

- Follow the repository's existing conventions and language
- Prefer small reusable components over duplicated inline SVG
- Keep brand tokens centralized
- Do not leave placeholder text or dead components
- Do not modify unrelated files
- Run formatting, linting, type-checking, tests and the production build using the repository's own commands
- Fix regressions introduced by the rebrand

VISUAL QA

Inspect the finished site at representative widths:
- 1440px desktop
- 1024px tablet/compact desktop
- 768px tablet
- 390px mobile
- 320px narrow mobile

Check:
- Logo clarity at navigation and favicon sizes
- No collisions between connecting lines and content
- Product line simplifies correctly on mobile
- Text contrast and focus visibility
- Reduced-motion behaviour
- Dark sections, if retained, use the one-colour white logo correctly
- No horizontal overflow

DELIVERABLE

After implementation, report:
1. What changed
2. Key files changed
3. Which old-brand references remain intentionally and why
4. Tests and builds run, with results
5. Any content or legal-name decisions requiring human confirmation
6. Screenshots or preview locations for desktop and mobile if the environment supports them

The finished site should feel like a rigorous enterprise technology company: spacious, mathematical and calm. Every point, line and shape should have a job. The central visual story is that Linear Horizon turns many signals and complex systems into one clear, actionable outcome.
```
